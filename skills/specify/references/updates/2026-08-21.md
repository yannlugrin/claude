# 2026-08-21 — four entries from one bootstrap's CI step

All four come from one operator report, written after a foundation `003`
step — the one that puts the existing local entry points onto the forge.
Two failures, and both survived the standing review gates: the step's cold
code review *raised* the first as an alternative and the implementer
dismissed it with a rule-shaped justification, and *argued* the second
itself, correctly against the tree it had read.

The pin half is not a first offence: it is the second consecutive project
where the operator had to ask for the bump bot after the digests had
already landed. Two projects reaching the same wrong place from the same
correct instruction is a property of the instruction, which is why U-067
is written at the pinning clause rather than as CI advice.

U-068 and U-070 are the general lessons underneath the two failures, and
they are the reason both were invisible to review: one was a misapplication
of rule 11 that read as a considered trade-off, and the other was a correct
finding applied to a tree that had moved since the reviewer read it.

Two amendments the report proposed are **not** banked, both declined by the
operator. *"When proportion argues for deletion the burden of proof is on
the deletion"* — U-068 and U-070 cover both observed failures, and stated
flatly it reverses rule 11's own force ("deletion is a legitimate outcome";
"a review round that only ever adds is how a small job becomes a large
one"), which was itself written against a real pattern. And *a second cold
review over the fix diff*, which would have caught both: a real gate on
every code-bearing step, where U-070 is the same insight for the price of a
sentence. Revisit if a third instance of that shape appears.

## U-067 — A digest pin lands with its bump bot, and the bot's runs are bounded

- **Applies to:** running
- **Change:** three parts of one mechanism, in two places.

  *The pinning clause states the pin's other half.* A pin is half a
  mechanism and the other half is what bumps it — not additional coverage,
  which that clause deliberately refuses for a third-party binary, but the
  rest of the same decision. A version a human reads may be bumped by a
  human. A digest a human cannot read — an action SHA, an image digest, a
  lock file — takes its automated bump in the step that takes the pin, or
  the pin is not taken. Where the forge offers no bot, the pin still names
  its bump trigger, logged at the pin.

  Reasoning: an unbumped digest fails *more quietly* than the floating tag
  it replaced. The tag silently stays current; the pin silently rots, while
  looking more deliberate than what it replaced. The two are one decision,
  and shipping the half with the worse failure mode is the error U-068
  names in general.

  *Step `003` says where it bites.* A forge's actions are published behind
  mutable major tags, so what CI ran is recorded by no diff in the
  repository — the reason to pin by digest, and the reason the pin cannot
  be taken alone.

  *And the bot is itself a CI producer* — the third part. It opens pull
  requests and force-pushes their branches as it rebases them, so
  superseded runs stop being hypothetical the moment it exists, and the
  workflow bounds them: queued runs per ref collapse to the latest, except
  on the branch whose green run is the record that an approved step passed,
  which is never cancelled. **The split is the part that must be stated** —
  a bound that cancels the step-close run destroys the evidence rule 6
  closes on, which is worse than no bound. Stated as behaviour, with the
  spelling taken from the forge in use (GitHub's `concurrency:` key at the
  time of writing).

  Why the bound lands at `003` and not earlier: in a single-operator
  repository whose pushes are approved steps, superseded runs are rare, and
  a bound written ahead of the bot is exactly rule 11's anticipation. The
  bot is what dates the need — which is why a reviewer applying proportion
  to the block before the bot existed was right about the tree it read
  (U-070).
- **Detect:** a workflow pinning any third-party step by SHA with no bump
  configuration in the repository; a bump configuration present with no
  concurrency bound on the workflow it feeds; a concurrency bound that
  cancels in-progress runs on the branch the step-close tag is cut from; or
  a decision entry deferring the bump as "machinery at the moment of need".
- **Remedy:** land the bump configuration and the bound together, as one
  change; where a decision entry deferred the bot, supersede it rather than
  amending it, citing this id. Where the workflow pins by mutable tag and
  the project would rather stay there, that is a live option — the rule
  forbids the half, not either whole.

## U-068 — Deferring half a mechanism is not proportion

- **Applies to:** running
- **Change:** rule 11's *build at the moment of need* bullet gains its
  precondition. The rule applies to things that are **separable**. Before
  deferring half of a mechanism, ask what the shipped half does alone: if
  it is worse than what preceded it, the two halves are one decision and
  both land now.

  Reasoning: proportion is the only rule in the set that argues for *less*,
  which makes it the one whose misapplication comes pre-justified. The
  observed case dismissed a bump bot as "machinery arrives at the moment of
  need (rule 11)" and cited the rule by number — a dismissal that read as a
  considered trade-off to the review that saw it, because it was
  rule-shaped. What was actually deferred was not a separable convenience
  but the half of a two-part mechanism without which the other half is a
  regression.
- **Detect:** a decision entry deferring a component under rule 11 whose
  counterpart shipped in the same step; more generally, any "not built yet,
  by proportion" whose absence changes the behaviour of what *was* built.
- **Remedy:** rule 11 in `CLAUDE.md` gains the clause. Deferrals already
  logged are not swept — they are re-read at the next step that touches
  what they deferred.

## U-069 — A reference's shape is taken, its text never is

- **Applies to:** running (projects with `{{REFERENCES}}` filled)
- **Change:** rule 3's read-only rule for `.claude/refs/` gains its other
  direction. Read-only has covered what flows *into* a reference — never
  edited, extended, annotated, compacted or deleted. It now covers what
  flows *out*: what is taken from a reference is its **shape**, never its
  text. Shape is a decision reachable independently — two parallel jobs, a
  cache keyed on the file that decides its contents. Text is the sentence
  explaining the decision, whatever its subject.

  And the boundary is a step rather than a judgement, because it is
  invisible at the moment of writing: **read the reference, close it, write
  the artifact without it open, and before committing compare the two — any
  sentence they share is text and comes out.** Shape survives paraphrase;
  text is what would have had to be quoted.

  Reasoning: the doctrine asked for CI "in the shape of the user's existing
  workflows" and never named a content boundary at all. The observed step
  produced a workflow whose comment blocks were the reference's prose with
  local substitutions, roughly 35 lines, written without awareness — the
  reference had been read at the start of the step and its sentences came
  back as fluent recall. The same step had stripped that reference from the
  published history under U-061, on the grounds that another project's
  material does not belong in this repository's install channel, while
  committing its prose into a file that ships. That is what makes this more
  than style: a reference is someone else's material, and prose surviving in
  a shipped file defeats the publish-or-strip ruling silently.
- **Detect:** a shipped file whose comments or prose match a file under
  `.claude/refs/` sentence for sentence; a step that ruled a reference
  stripped from publication while committing text derived from it.
- **Remedy:** rewrite the shared sentences from the artifact's own facts, in
  the repository's own voice. Where the reference was ruled stripped,
  treat surviving prose as part of that ruling's scope, not as a separate
  question.

## U-070 — A review's findings are true of the tree it read

- **Applies to:** running
- **Change:** the handover ritual's review step states what a finding is
  true *of*. Findings describe the tree the reviewer read, and fixes move
  that tree — so before applying one, check it still holds against the tree
  as it now stands, including changes made in answer to the same review.
  Any finding whose justification is *nothing needs this* has that claim
  re-derived rather than inherited: a deletion argued on absence of need is
  the finding most likely to have expired.

  Reasoning: every fix pass has this shape — the review reads commit N, the
  fixes land at N+k, and findings correct at N are applied at N+k without
  re-derivation. Deletions are the dangerous subset, because "nothing needs
  this" is precisely the claim later work invalidates. Observed: a reviewer
  argued, correctly for what it saw, that a concurrency block solved a
  problem this repository had never had; the need arrived in the same fix
  pass, in the bump bot added answering an earlier finding from the same
  report. Neither party could see it — the reviewer read a tree without the
  bot, and the implementer did not re-examine deletions the earlier pass had
  argued for.
- **Detect:** a step whose review fixes deleted something on absence-of-need
  grounds while the same pass added a mechanism; more cheaply, an
  instantiated `handover-step` whose review step says only "address or
  rebut each finding".
- **Remedy:** amend the instantiated `handover-step`. No sweep of past
  steps — the rule binds at the next review.
